Hypothesis:
	In a bounded region watched by ground-based line-of-sight sensors, least-exposure routes between any two points for a low-altitude vehicle (such as a drone) pass through terrain with significantly lower relative elevation (valleys, leeside of ridges, etc) and higher local relief than shortest-distance routes between the same endpoints. This effect holds across randomized sensor layouts and weakens as flight altitude (AGL) increases.







Caveats:
	- Bare-earth DEM (Digital Elevation Model) ignores trees/buildings
	- Line-of-Sight does not necessarily indicate detection, just possibilty
	- Perfect attacker knowledge may not always be possible in real world scenarios
	- Turn-radius/climb-rate limits are not considered. Smoothing can be applied to the paths to decrease, but not eliminate, this concern
	- A feature must be deeper than flight altitude to conceal vehicle
